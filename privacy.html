Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 October 2026

Short version

Picso processes your photo library on your device. We do not collect, transmit, sell or store your photos or your personal data on any server. We have no server that holds your data, because the app is built to not need one. There is no account to link to you.

What Picso accesses, and why

Each permission below is asked for by iOS the first time a feature needs it. You can say no, or change your mind later in the iOS Settings app; only the feature that needs it stops working.

  • Photo library — read to find duplicates, near-identical and burst shots, blurry photos, old screenshots and large videos, and to build the app's local index. Picso only changes your library when you ask it to: deleting what you confirm in the system dialog, restoring from the vault, or filing a photo into an album you pick. Filing into albums needs full library access; with limited access the rest of the app still works.
  • Photos stored in iCloud — if you use iCloud Photos and open a photo whose original is not on the device, iOS downloads it from Apple's iCloud for you. That transfer is between your device and Apple; Picso never receives a copy anywhere else. Background scans never trigger downloads and skip such photos.
  • Contacts (only if you use contact cleanup) — read on the device to find duplicate cards you can merge. Nothing changes until you confirm a merge, and contacts are never copied out of your phone.
  • Face ID or passcode (only if you turn on the vault lock) — handled entirely by iOS. Picso only learns whether unlocking succeeded; it never sees your face data or passcode.
  • Photo details — dates, sizes and, when a photo has one, its location are read from the photo itself so the info panel can show them. They are not stored anywhere new or sent anywhere.
  • People grouping — to let you review photos of the same person together, Picso compares crops of the people in your photos on the device. It does not identify anyone, does not use face recognition, and builds no profile of a person.
  • App storage on the device — the local index and scan cache, your settings, your Decide Later and Skip lists, and the vault (below).
  • StoreKit (Apple) — to check whether Picso Pro is active on this device.
  • UserDefaults and available disk space — declared in the app's privacy manifest for ordinary app functioning: remembering your settings, and refusing to write when the volume is full, and measuring how much free space a cleanup gave back. That measurement is taken on the device and is not sent anywhere.

The vault

With Picso Pro, the original of each photo or video is copied into a vault inside the app before it is deleted from your library, so you can restore it. The vault lives only on your device and is excluded from iCloud and computer backups, so a copy of what you deleted does not linger in a backup. Vault items are removed automatically after the retention period you choose in Settings (90 days unless you change it), or straight away when you empty the vault. You can lock the vault with Face ID or your passcode. Vault files are stored with iOS complete file protection, so they stay encrypted while the device is locked. You can also export vault items to the Files app; where they go from there is up to you. Deleting Picso deletes the vault with it.

Widgets, Shortcuts and background scans

  • Widgets show counts only (for example how many screenshots or duplicates are waiting, and how much space you could free). The app shares those numbers with its widget through a container on your device; no photos or file names go there.
  • Shortcuts and Siri actions just open Picso on the matching screen. They pass no photos or library data to other apps.
  • Background scans — if iOS allows background refresh, Picso may update its local index overnight, using data already on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

What Picso does not do

  • No upload of photos, thumbnails, contacts, faces, locations, captions or index data. Picso has no server and makes no network requests of its own.
  • No third-party analytics, no advertising SDK, no crash-reporting service. The app ships Apple's own frameworks only.
  • No tracking across other apps or web sites, so no App Tracking Transparency prompt is needed.
  • No account, so no email address, name or password is collected.

Opt-in diagnostics

Picso can record local diagnostic information about scans it performs. This is off unless you turn it on in Settings, it stays on the device, and it is not transmitted. Turning it off and clearing app data removes it.

Children

Picso is a general-audience utility, not directed at children, and it collects no personal data from anyone.

Your rights, in practice

Because we hold nothing about you, there is nothing for us to export, correct or delete. Your own copy is under your control: delete Picso to remove everything it stored, or delete individual items from inside the app. If you want the App Store listing to reflect a data-safety declaration, the truthful one for this build is Data Not Collected.

Purchases

Picso is free to use with up to 50 deletions a day. Picso Pro is an optional yearly subscription bought through the App Store. Payment is processed by Apple. Neither we nor anyone acting for us sees your card number, and your receipts live in your Apple Account. Picso reads only the entitlement StoreKit reports for this device.

Changes

If Picso ever changes this posture — for example by adding a service that receives data — this page is updated first, with a new date at the top, before that version of the app is released.
