Nesto privacy policy

Version 2, 20 October 2026

Who we are

Nesto is made and published by Babak Armani, an individual (not a company), Belpbergstrasse 26B, 3110 Münsingen, Switzerland. Contact for anything about your data: supportnow_nesto@pm.me. Babak is the controller for the processing described here, to the extent Nesto determines it.

Nesto receives no personal data

We, the publisher, do not receive or store any of your personal data. Your entries stay on your iPhone, or in your own iCloud if you turn on sharing or backup. We cannot see them there. Apple handles any purchase; we never see your name or card details. If you write to us, we use your email only to answer you and then delete it.

The short version

  • Nesto has no Nesto account and no Nesto server. Your Apple ID is the only account.
  • Your entries stay on your iPhone. They go to Apple's iCloud only if you share a child with someone, or if you turn on "Keep my children in iCloud" (off by default).
  • What goes to iCloud is stored in your own iCloud account (and, for a shared child, is visible to the people you invited). We, the publisher, cannot see it.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics tools, no selling of data.
  • Nesto is a parenting guide and family diary. It is not a medical app and does not store health records.

What Nesto stores

  • Your child's first name, birth date and country, so the app can show content for their age and country.
  • Your care logs: sleep times, feeds (breast side and duration, bottle amount and milk type, solid foods), nappies (wet or dirty), and the date each tooth appears or a milk tooth falls out, each with an optional note.
  • Diary moments ("firsts") with text and photos.
  • Reminders you set (kept on your iPhone as local notifications).
  • If you share a child: the people you invited and the role you gave them (parent, caregiver or viewer). Their names or Apple ID contact details are shown to you by iCloud, not by us.
  • If you subscribe to Nesto Plus: whether your subscription is active. Apple handles the purchase and payment. The status is checked by your iPhone with Apple and stays there; no purchase data, name or card details reach us.

Nesto does not collect your location, contacts, advertising identifier or usage analytics, and contains no third-party analytics or advertising code.

Crash reports

Nesto contains no crash-reporting tool of its own. If you turned on "Share with App Developers" in iOS Settings, Apple may pass us anonymous crash reports and diagnostics under Apple's own terms. You can turn this off at any time in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

All entry fields Nesto puts in iCloud are stored as CloudKit encrypted fields, and larger photos as encrypted files (assets). Apple's iCloud data security overview says that "Third-party app data stored in iCloud is always encrypted in transit and on server." With Apple's standard data protection, Apple keeps the encryption keys in its data centers, so Apple can decrypt the data on your behalf (for example to help you recover it). If you turn on Apple's Advanced Data Protection, the same page says "CloudKit encrypted fields and assets are end-to-end encrypted", with keys only on your trusted devices. The same page says that "Both Apple and third-party data centers may be used to store and process your data."

Who can see your data

  • You, on every iPhone signed in to your Apple ID that runs Nesto.
  • People you invite to a shared child: they see and (unless you made them a viewer) add to that child's logs, diary moments and photos. Only the parent who shared the child can invite or remove people.
  • Apple, which provides iCloud to you under your own agreement with Apple. Apple's iCloud terms (section II.D "Third-Party Apps") say that Apple collects, stores and processes data that apps store in iCloud on behalf of the app developer. For your Apple ID, iCloud account and storage plan, Apple acts under its own privacy policy.
  • Not us. Nothing goes to a Nesto server: there is none. The publisher cannot open your iCloud data.

International transfers

Apple may store iCloud data in Apple or third-party data centers outside Switzerland and the EEA. These transfers happen under your agreement with Apple and Apple's data transfer safeguards.

Why and on what legal basis

We process this data to provide the app you asked for: the guide, the diary, sharing and backup when you turn them on (GDPR art. 6(1)(b), performance of a contract; in Switzerland, processing under the nFADP to fulfil our contract with you). Sharing and "Keep my children in iCloud" happen only when you turn them on. You enter data about your child as their parent or legal guardian.

How long we keep it

  • On your iPhone: until you delete it.
  • In iCloud: until you delete it in Nesto, turn off "Keep my children in iCloud" (for children you have not shared), or stop sharing. Apple's own retention of deleted iCloud data and backups follows Apple's terms; we do not control it.
  • People you removed from a shared child, or who left, lose access to that child's iCloud area at once. What their iPhone had already received stays on their iPhone, and Nesto tells you so when you remove someone.

How to delete your data

  • Delete one entry: it disappears from every iPhone that shares the child, and its content (text and photo) is removed from iCloud.
  • Delete a child (Settings › the child › Delete): removes the child from this iPhone. If you own the child, its iCloud area and any share are deleted too, so it also disappears for everyone you invited. If someone else shared the child with you, your iPhone leaves the share; the child stays with its owner.
  • Leave a share: to leave a child someone shared with you, delete that child in Nesto on your iPhone (see above). If the owner removed you, Nesto offers "Stop sharing on this iPhone"; entries already on your iPhone stay there until you delete the child.
  • Remove someone from a shared child (owner only): they lose access to the child's iCloud area at once; what their iPhone already received stays on it.
  • Turn off "Keep my children in iCloud": children you have not shared are deleted from your iCloud. They stay on this iPhone.
  • Delete all data (Settings › Delete all data): removes all children, logs, photos and reminders from this iPhone and from your iCloud, and leaves every shared child. This cannot be undone.
  • Deleting the app removes the data on that iPhone. It does not remove what is in iCloud: use Delete all data first, or manage Nesto's iCloud data in iOS Settings › [your name] › iCloud.

Children

Nesto is used by parents and caregivers. Children are the people the diary is about; they do not use the app themselves. A parent decides what is recorded about their child and who it is shared with.

Your rights

Because we hold none of your data on our own systems, you can exercise most rights directly in the app: view and correct every entry, export everything as a file (Settings › Export all data), and delete as described above. You can also write to supportnow_nesto@pm.me.

  • Switzerland (nFADP): you have the right to information about your data (art. 25), to receive or have it transferred (art. 28), and to have it corrected or deleted and to object to processing (art. 32). You can complain to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC).
  • Austria and Liechtenstein (GDPR): you have the right of access (art. 15), rectification (art. 16), erasure (art. 17), restriction (art. 18), data portability (art. 20) and to object (art. 21). You can complain to a supervisory authority (art. 77), in particular the Austrian Datenschutzbehörde or the Datenschutzstelle Liechtenstein.

We answer requests within 30 days. Because we cannot see your iCloud data, we may need you to use the in-app tools to act on it.

Security

Data on your iPhone is protected by iOS file encryption and an optional Face ID or passcode lock in the app. Data in iCloud is protected by Apple as described above; turning on Advanced Data Protection in iOS makes it end-to-end encrypted.

Changes

We will tell you in the app before this policy changes in a way that matters.
